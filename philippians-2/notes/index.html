The Mind of Christ

Philippians 2:3–8

Coffee and the Word at Jim's · Friday, November 13, 2026

Luke 18 showed us the low place. Philippians 2 shows us who went there first. Paul writes to a church he loves and tells them to think the way Jesus thinks, then walks them down from glory to the cross.

Companion notes to the Philippians 2 study guide.

Key Takeaways

  • Humility in this passage is about attention. It means looking to the things of others, not only your own (v. 4).
  • Paul doesn't ground the command in good manners. He grounds it in Christ (v. 5).
  • Verses 6 through 8 move in one direction: down. The Son of God gives up His standing, takes a servant's form, and obeys to the point of death.

Setting the Scene

Paul writes this letter in bonds (Philippians 1:13). Acts 16:12 calls Philippi a colony, a Roman city in Macedonia. The scholar Joseph Hellerman argues that few cities in the Greek East cared more about rank and public honor. If he's right, a call to count others better than yourself cut straight against how that city worked.

The Command

Philippians 2:3–4KJV

3Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves. 4Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others.

Notice the two things Paul rules out: strife and vainglory. One is pushing others down. The other is lifting yourself up. Both keep score. Then he gives the alternative, and it's practical. Look up from your own things.

The Mind of Christ

Philippians 2:5–8KJV

5Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus: 6Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: 7But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: 8And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.

Paul could have listed more rules. He points to a person. Jesus was in the form of God, and He did not treat that standing as something to grab and hold. He went down, and He kept going down.

The steps down

  • He made Himself of no reputation.
  • He took the form of a servant.
  • He was made in the likeness of men.
  • He humbled Himself.
  • He became obedient unto death.
  • It was the death of the cross.

Word Studies

"Lowliness of mind"ταπεινοφροσύνῃ

tapeinophrosynē · Philippians 2:3

A compound of low and mind: humility of mind. In a city that prized rank, Paul holds it up as the way to live.

"Made himself of no reputation"ἐκένωσεν

ekenōsen · Philippians 2:7

The verb literally means to empty. Paul explains the emptying with what follows: Christ took the form of a servant. He didn't give up being God. He took the low place.

"Servant"δούλου

doulou · Philippians 2:7

This is the word for a slave. The KJV usually renders it servant. Paul uses the same word for himself and Timothy in Philippians 1:1. The One who was in the form of God took the form of a slave.
